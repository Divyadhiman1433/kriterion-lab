# Presentation Structure

The presentation contains one title slide followed by five content slides. Each slide answers one question and uses complete sentences rather than production labels or promotional language.

## Title Slide

The university name and mark appear above the project title. The remaining information is limited to the study description, author, roll number, and course context.

## Slide 1 — Study Design

The slide explains why two datasets are used, how training and test data are separated, and how feature selection and optimisation are compared under the same experimental conditions.

## Slide 2 — Feature Selection Result

The slide shows that recursive feature elimination reduced the classification input from 30 features to 10 while retaining the baseline test accuracy.

## Slide 3 — Linear Regression Result

The slide compares the OLS fit with batch gradient descent and explains why matching coefficients validate the iterative implementation.

## Slide 4 — Logistic Regression Result

The slide presents the ROC curve, confusion matrix, and classification metrics for the RFE subset. It also states the role of sigmoid activation and binary cross-entropy in one sentence.

## Slide 5 — Interpretation and Limitations

The final slide separates conclusions supported by the experiment from claims the experiment cannot establish. It closes with the non-diagnostic scope of the project.

## Visual Direction

The deck uses a warm off-white background, navy text, teal rules, and burgundy only where contrast is useful. Charts remain the primary visual evidence. There are no visible source footers, slogans, badges, dashboard cards, or internal presentation labels.
